# Frame Real Product

Turn intent into a narrow, testable product claim before choosing architecture.

## Establish evidence

Inspect available user research, workflow artifacts, product code, support notes, and metrics. Label each claim as:

- observed recent behaviour;
- measured system fact;
- domain-informed hypothesis;
- unverified assumption;
- decision already made.

Do not promote familiarity into evidence. Let recent users and operating facts override inherited intuition.

## Write one picture

Describe one person in one moment:

> At **time/situation**, **person** needs to **job** while **pressure**. Today they **observable workaround**. A useful result lets them **verifiable outcome**.

Reject audience words such as “users,” “businesses,” or “analysts” unless the role and moment are concrete.

## Choose the wedge

Name the smallest recurring job worth solving end to end. Test it:

1. Does the behaviour occur now?
2. Is the pain visible and repeated?
3. Can the user verify the result?
4. Can the first version avoid an unnecessary high-risk decision?
5. Would five actual people choose it over their workaround?

Write explicit non-goals and refusal boundaries. If the wedge requires solving several unrelated jobs, narrow it.

## Build a behaviour ledger

For each critical situation, record:

| Situation | Observable behaviour | Pressure/failure | Product obligation | Falsifiable check |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Include defaults, empty state, first use, interruption, correction, and final-minute use. Describe behaviour rather than imagined preference.

## Separate evidence and authority

Identify:

- facts and definition owners;
- assumptions and unresolved questions;
- data allowed and data excluded;
- system recommendations, if any;
- the person who retains the decision;
- actions requiring explicit approval.

Prefer an exception path to a forced answer.

## Define the pilot

Predeclare:

- eligible users and moments;
- success measure;
- serious-error threshold;
- stop/kill condition;
- expiry/review date;
- evidence needed to expand scope.

Do not call activity, positive feedback, or polished output proof of value by itself.

## Deliver

Produce, in this order:

1. One-picture statement.
2. Wedge and explicit non-goals.
3. Evidence/assumption table.
4. Behaviour ledger.
5. Data and decision boundary.
6. Lowest suitable architecture hypothesis.
7. Pilot gates and kill criteria.
8. Open questions with owners.

Use plain professional language. State what is known, inferred, and undecided.
